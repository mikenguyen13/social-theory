# Launch kit

Ready-to-post copy and a checklist for announcing the book. Nothing here is
published automatically — review, edit to your voice, and post yourself.
Replace `https://01a17b63-f04b-c542-bc6c-ece12120ea40.share.connect.posit.cloud` with the live link if it is not already filled in.

No one can guarantee virality. What reliably helps: a clear one-line promise,
a strong visual (use `images/social-card.png`), posting where the audience
already gathers, timing (Tue–Thu mornings, US Eastern), replying to every
comment in the first two hours, and a repo that converts visitors into stars
(README, topics, social preview, pinned repo).

## One-line pitch

> A free, open-source field guide to 100+ social science theories — each with a
> theory card, mechanism diagram, evidence & critiques, business cases, and
> ready-to-use research hypotheses.

## X / Bluesky / Threads thread

1. I rewrote my open-source book on social science theory from scratch.
   100+ theories, effects & frameworks — free to read. 🧵
   https://01a17b63-f04b-c542-bc6c-ece12120ea40.share.connect.posit.cloud
2. The problem: every field has theories its members "just know" — signaling,
   agency, RBV, prospect theory, social identity — and PhD students are expected
   to absorb them one paper at a time.
3. Every theory gets a "theory card": core claim, origins, level of analysis,
   constructs, boundary conditions, related theories. Compare theories at a glance.
4. Then: core logic & propositions, a mechanism diagram, the evidence, the
   critiques (incl. replication status), and business cases.
5. Each major theory ends with a research toolkit: sample hypotheses, measures,
   designs, and data sources. Built for dissertation writing.
6. There's an interactive theory map (100+ nodes, linked by cross-references)
   and a searchable Theory Atlas you can download as CSV.
7. Every figure is reproducible R code. Contributions welcome — suggest a theory
   or fix an error on GitHub: https://github.com/mikenguyen13/social-theory
8. If it's useful, a ⭐ on GitHub or a share helps other students find it. 🙏
